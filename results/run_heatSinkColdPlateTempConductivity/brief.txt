Cold-plate-cooled power-module spreader with temperature-dependent conductivity: the nonlinear version of the cold-plate benchmark, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of the spreader plate of a liquid-cooled power module scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. A power chip occupying the central forty percent of the bottom edge injects a uniform heat flux of one thousand watts per square metre (0.1 W/cm^2); the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic. The bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the chip position is prescribed). The TOP edge — opposite the chip — is the wall of a cooling pipe (a cold plate, i.e. a liquid-cooled cold rail): it is held at the prescribed reference temperature of zero kelvin, the zero of the temperature scale, an isothermal heat sink of negligible thermal resistance. The left and right edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin. The thermal conductivity of the aluminium spreader is TEMPERATURE-DEPENDENT: k(u) = k0 (1 + beta (u - u_amb)) with k0 = 200 W/(m K) and the linear temperature coefficient beta = 5e-4 1/K, a realistic order of magnitude for aluminium between 270 kelvin and 340 kelvin (the conductivity varies by about plus or minus 2 percent across the working temperature span); the k(u) dependence makes the state problem NONLINEAR and is the physical mechanism studied here. This is the cross-section of an electric-vehicle traction-inverter module: the dies load the baseplate, the opposite face is bolted to a water-glycol cold plate, and the sides see the air inside the housing. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the temperature-dependent conductivity k(u) of aluminium, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 W/(m K), i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the temperature integral over the chip — the thermal compliance that measures the thermal resistance of the spreader. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement. The initial design is an I-beam without holes: a full-width top flange (thickness 0.2) lying along the cooling wall, a bottom flange (length 0.5, thickness 0.2) under the chip footprint, and a web of width 0.2 running the full height between them. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements with the cooling-wall temperature applied directly to the wall segment; the remaining edges carry convective conditions. The nonlinear state equation is solved by Newton's method, whose linearisation is derived automatically.
