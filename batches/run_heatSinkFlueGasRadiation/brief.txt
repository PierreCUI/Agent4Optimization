Convectively-and-radiatively-heated heat-sink topology optimization in two dimensions: the nonlinear version of the flue-gas benchmark, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1], the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], and the radiative heat-transfer theory of Howell, Siegel & Menguc (2010) [3]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of the conducting wall of a flue-gas heat-recovery duct scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. Low-temperature exhaust gas at 338 kelvin sweeps the central forty percent of the bottom edge and injects heat by TWO parallel mechanisms: convection through a Robin condition with a heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin, AND gas radiation — the wall absorbs the radiative flux q_rad = eps_g sigma (u_H^4 - u^4) emitted by the hot gas, where eps_g = 0.9 is the effective emissivity of the gas-filled duct and the oxidised wall and sigma = 5.67e-8 W/(m^2 K^4) is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant; when the wall is cold the radiative flux reaches eps_g sigma u_H^4 ≈ 666 W/m^2, of the same order as the convective load 1000 W/m^2. The remaining bottom edge is adiabatic and the bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the gas-side footprint is prescribed). The other three edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin, representative of the duct casing in ambient air. The u^4 dependence of the absorbed flux makes the state problem NONLINEAR and is the physical mechanism studied here. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the gas-side surface-temperature integral weighted by q_b = h_H (u_H - u_amb) = 25 x 40 = 1000 W/m^2 — the heat the heated side would absorb if it reached ambient temperature. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement. The initial design is the full solid square with a circular hole of radius 0.1 cut out of its centre. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements; there is no Dirichlet condition, the temperature is fixed implicitly by the ambient air through the Robin condition. The nonlinear state equation is solved by Newton's method, whose linearisation is derived automatically.
