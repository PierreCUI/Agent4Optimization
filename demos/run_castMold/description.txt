Flow through a Casting Runner System under Gravity

This is the runner system problem of metal casting: the molten metal
is poured into the mould through a sprue on top, and it must be guided
through the runner channels to the outlet gates, gravity pulling the
metal downward all the way, and the task is to lay out the runner
system inside the mould so that the metal fills it with the least
dissipated energy. The mould is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is fluid. The physics is steady, linear Stokes
flow with one single physical field, the velocity u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres per second; the
pressure is eliminated by enforcing incompressibility with a large
penalty on the velocity divergence, and the solid regions are modelled
as an impermeable porous medium through a Darcy damping term that
resists the flow, so the flow naturally carves its channel through the
fluid regions. The fluid has the dynamic viscosity 1 N s/m^2 and the
incompressibility penalty is 10000 N s/m^2; the solid applies a Darcy
resistance of 10000 N s/m^4 while the fluid applies none. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern phi(x,y) = min(min(min((x-0.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-2.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04), min((x-3.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-0.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04)), min((x-2.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-3.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, 10.0)), negative where the material is solid and positive where it is fluid, with about half the domain initially solid.

The top edge is split into segments: the central segment, between the
horizontal positions 1.5 and 2.5, is the sprue inlet, where the fluid
enters with a uniform prescribed velocity of 1 m/s pointing downward
into the domain, both velocity components fixed, while the remaining
parts of the top edge on both sides of the sprue are no-slip walls,
both velocity components zero. The bottom edge is split into segments:
two outlet gates, the first between the positions 0.5 and 1.2 and the
second between the positions 2.8 and 3.5, are outlets, where the flow
leaves freely with zero traction, while the remaining parts of the
bottom edge are no-slip walls, both velocity components zero. The left
edge and the right edge are no-slip walls, both velocity components
zero. Besides the inflow, the fluid is pulled downward by its own
weight, a uniform body force of 9810 N/m^3 acting throughout the
domain, representing gravity. The sprue inlet, the outlet gates and all
the wall segments are non-design boundaries: their positions are
prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on them; the material layout inside the domain is free. The
optimization goal is to minimize the total energy dissipated by the
flow through the runner system — the viscous dissipation of the fluid
plus the Darcy damping of the solid regions — while using as little
material as possible, a balance between flow performance and material
cost.
