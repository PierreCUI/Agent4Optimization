Solar Panel under Sun Heating, Snow Load and Self-Weight

This is the solar panel problem of photovoltaic installations: the
panel absorbs sunlight and heats up uniformly through its thickness,
sheds the heat by convection to the air on both faces, and carries the
snow and wind load and its own weight on the mounting rails. The panel
is a long strip whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is
treated as plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady: linear heat
conduction for the temperature T, measured in kelvin, and linear
elasticity for the displacement u, a vector with a horizontal and a
vertical component, measured in metres; the two are coupled one-way —
the temperature drives a thermal expansion strain of 3.6e-5 per kelvin
times the temperature, subtracted from the elastic strain, so a heated
solid region expands and pushes against its supports. The solid
material has Young's modulus 3.2e7 N/m^2, Poisson's ratio 0.23, density
2500 kg/m^3, thermal conductivity 1.25 W/(m K) and thermal expansion
coefficient 3.6e-5 per kelvin; regions without material are treated as
a weak phantom that is 1000 times softer, 1000 times less conductive
and 1000 times less expansive than the solid. The optimization starts
from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and
void cells covering the rectangle, roughly half solid, with a thin
solid strip along the top edge.

Heat is generated uniformly inside every solid region, with a
volumetric heat source of 300 W/m^3 everywhere in the material, the
absorbed solar radiation. The top edge and the bottom edge lose heat by
convection to the surrounding air at temperature 20, with a heat
transfer coefficient h of 10 W/(m^2 K), the front glass and the back
sheet cooling to the air; the top edge at the same time carries a
uniform traction of 5000 N/m^2 pressing downward, the snow and wind
load, while the bottom edge is free of any mechanical load. The left
edge and the right edge are the frame rails: each is held in place by
strong penalty springs that pull its horizontal and vertical
displacements back to zero, for the panel is bolted onto the rails, and
each is insulated, so that no heat crosses it. Besides the snow load,
the solid material is pulled downward by its own weight, a uniform body
force of 24500 N/m^3 acting throughout the volume of every solid
region. The sun-lit top face and the two frame rails are non-design
boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the convecting back
face is a design boundary, where material may be attached or removed
freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy
stored in the panel under the snow load, the solar heating and its own
weight — its compliance — while using as little material as possible, a
balance between stiffness and material cost, and a strong penalty keeps
the frame rails from sliding horizontally.
