Flow through a Pipe Elbow under a Uniform Inlet

This is the pipe elbow problem of chemical plants and HVAC ducting: a
fluid enters a straight pipe and is turned through a ninety-degree
bend, where the flow separates at the inner corner and wastes energy
in recirculation; the task is to shape the material inside the bend so
that the fluid turns with the least possible dissipation. The duct is
a long prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is
treated in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is fluid. The physics is steady, linear Stokes
flow with one single physical field, the velocity u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres per second; the
pressure is eliminated by enforcing incompressibility with a large
penalty on the velocity divergence, and the solid regions are modelled
as an impermeable porous medium through a Darcy damping term that
resists the flow, so the flow naturally carves its channel through the
fluid regions. The fluid has the dynamic viscosity 1 N s/m^2 and the
incompressibility penalty is 10000 N s/m^2; the solid applies a Darcy
resistance of 10000 N s/m^4 while the fluid applies none. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern phi(x,y) = min(min(min((x-0.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-2.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04), min((x-3.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-0.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04)), min((x-2.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-3.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, 10.0)), negative where the material is solid and positive where it is fluid, with about half the domain initially solid.

The left edge is the inlet: the fluid enters through it with a uniform
prescribed velocity of 1 m/s pointing into the domain, both velocity
components fixed. The bottom edge is split: its left half, between the
horizontal positions 0 and 2, is a no-slip wall, both velocity
components zero, and its right half, between the positions 2 and 4, is
the outlet, where the flow leaves freely with zero traction. The right
edge is a no-slip wall, both velocity components zero. The top edge is
a no-slip wall, both velocity components zero. There is no body force.
The inlet, the outlet and the right wall are non-design boundaries:
their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer
cannot add or remove material on them; the top wall is a design
boundary, where material may be attached or removed freely. The
optimization goal is to minimize the total energy dissipated by the
flow through the bend — the viscous dissipation of the fluid plus the
Darcy damping of the solid regions — while using as little material as
possible, a balance between flow performance and material cost.
