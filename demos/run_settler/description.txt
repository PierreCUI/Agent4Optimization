Flow through a Grit Settler under Gravity

This is the grit settler problem of wastewater treatment: the
suspended grit-laden water enters a basin, flows slowly along it, and
the grit sinks to the bed under its own weight while the clean water
leaves at the far end, and the task is to lay out the material inside
the basin so that the flow spreads evenly and dissipates the least
energy under gravity. The basin is a long prism whose cross-section is
a rectangle, so the problem is treated in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is fluid. The physics is steady, linear Stokes
flow with one single physical field, the velocity u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres per second; the
pressure is eliminated by enforcing incompressibility with a large
penalty on the velocity divergence, and the solid regions are modelled
as an impermeable porous medium through a Darcy damping term that
resists the flow, so the flow naturally carves its channel through the
fluid regions. The fluid has the dynamic viscosity 1 N s/m^2 and the
incompressibility penalty is 10000 N s/m^2; the solid applies a Darcy
resistance of 10000 N s/m^4 while the fluid applies none. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern phi(x,y) = min(min(min((x-0.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-2.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04), min((x-3.5)^2+(y-0.25)^2-0.04, (x-0.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-1.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04)), min((x-2.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, (x-3.5)^2+(y-0.75)^2-0.04, 10.0)), negative where the material is solid and positive where it is fluid, with about half the domain initially solid.

The left edge is split into two segments: the upper segment, between
the heights 0.5 and 1, is the inlet, where the fluid enters with a
uniform prescribed velocity of 1 m/s pointing into the domain, both
velocity components fixed, while the lower segment, between the
heights 0 and 0.5, is a no-slip wall, both velocity components zero.
The right edge is split into two segments: the upper segment, between
the heights 0.5 and 1, is the outlet, where the flow leaves freely
with zero traction, while the lower segment, between the heights 0 and
0.5, is a no-slip wall, both velocity components zero. The bottom edge
is the bed of the basin, a no-slip wall, both velocity components
zero. The top edge is a no-slip wall, both velocity components zero.
Besides the inflow, the fluid is pulled downward by its own weight, a
uniform body force of 9810 N/m^3 acting throughout the domain,
representing gravity. The inlet, the outlet, the bed and the
neighbouring wall segments are non-design boundaries: their positions
are prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or
remove material on them; the top wall is a design boundary, where
material may be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is
to minimize the total energy dissipated by the flow through the
settler — the viscous dissipation of the fluid plus the Darcy damping
of the solid regions — while using as little material as possible, a
balance between flow performance and material cost.
