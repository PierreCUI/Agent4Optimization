Water-wall heat-recovery topology optimization in two dimensions, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], with the design-dependent convection load of Iga et al. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of the conducting wall of a flue-gas heat-recovery duct scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. Low-temperature exhaust gas at 338 kelvin sweeps the central forty percent of the bottom edge and injects heat by convection through a Robin condition with a heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin — the gas side of a condensing heat-recovery exchanger; the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic. The bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the gas-side footprint is prescribed). The TOP edge — opposite the gas side — is a water wall (a membrane-wall cooling-pipe surface): it is held at the prescribed reference temperature of zero kelvin, the zero of the temperature scale, an isothermal heat sink of negligible thermal resistance; the design must conduct the recovered heat from the gas side to this wall. The left and right edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin. This problem is the cold-plate heat-sink benchmark with its Neumann load boundary replaced by the Robin condition: the equivalent surface heat flux q_b = h_H (u_H - u_amb) = 25 x 40 = 1000 W/m^2 equals the benchmark flux q = 1000 W/m^2 by construction. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the gas-side surface-temperature integral weighted by q_b — the heat the heated side would absorb if it reached ambient temperature. The initial design is an I-beam (工字形) without holes: a full-width top flange (thickness 0.2) lying along the water wall, a bottom flange (length 0.5, thickness 0.2) under the gas-side footprint, and a web of width 0.2 running the full height between them. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements with the water-wall temperature applied directly to the wall segment; the remaining edges carry convective conditions.
