Gravity Dam with Confinement-Dependent Stiffness

This is the concrete gravity dam problem of hydropower and flood
control with the confinement behaviour of rock and concrete: a massive
wall of concrete holds back a reservoir by its own weight — the water
presses on the upstream face and grows heavier with depth, the dam's
weight compresses its body onto the rock foundation, and the concrete
stiffens where it is most compressed, exactly as rock and concrete do
under confinement. The dam is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid's Young's modulus depends on the local volumetric strain: it is
200 N/m^2 where the material neither compacts nor dilates, and it
doubles at a volumetric strain of one tenth — the modulus rises with
the square of the volumetric strain, so the rock stiffens whether it
is compacted or dilated, the confinement hardening of rock and
concrete; this dependence makes the state problem nonlinear. Regions
without material are treated as a weak
elastic solid 100 times softer than the solid at the same deformation
state. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a
checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the whole
rectangle, roughly half solid.

The left edge is the upstream face: the reservoir presses on it with a
water pressure that grows linearly with depth, zero at the top edge
and 10 N/m^2 at the bottom, pressing perpendicular to the face. The
bottom edge is the rock foundation: it is held completely fixed, its
horizontal and vertical displacements both zero. The top edge and the
right edge, the downstream face, are free. Besides the water pressure,
the solid material is pulled downward by its own weight, a uniform
body force of 10 N/m^3 acting throughout the volume of every solid
region. The pressurized upstream face and the fixed foundation are
non-design boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are
frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the
top edge and the downstream face are design boundaries, where material
may be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is to
minimize the elastic strain energy stored in the dam under the water
pressure and its own weight — its compliance — while using as little
material as possible, a balance between stiffness and material cost.
