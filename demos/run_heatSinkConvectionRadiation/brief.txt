Convection-and-radiation-cooled heat-sink topology optimization in two dimensions: the nonlinear version of the convection-cooled heat-sink benchmark, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1], the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], and the radiative heat-transfer theory of Howell, Siegel & Menguc (2010) [3]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of an aluminium heat sink scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. A power chip occupying the central forty percent of the bottom edge injects a uniform heat flux of one thousand watts per square metre (0.1 W/cm^2); the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic and the bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the chip position is prescribed). The other three edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin by TWO parallel mechanisms: convection through an effective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin, representative of a finned natural-convection heat sink, AND surface radiation following the Stefan-Boltzmann law q_rad = eps sigma (u^4 - u_amb^4), where eps = 0.8 is the emissivity of the oxidised aluminium surface and sigma = 5.67e-8 W/(m^2 K^4) is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. At the implied surface-temperature rise of a few kelvin the radiative flux is of order eps sigma (303^4 - 298^4) ≈ 13 W/m^2, about fifteen percent of the convective flux — the physically realistic share of radiation in natural-convection heat-sink design. The u^4 dependence makes the state problem NONLINEAR and is the physical mechanism studied here. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the temperature integral over the chip — the thermal compliance that measures the thermal resistance of the heat sink. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement. The initial design is the full solid square with a circular hole of radius 0.1 cut out of its centre. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements; there is no Dirichlet condition, the temperature is fixed implicitly by the ambient air through the Robin condition. The nonlinear state equation is solved by Newton's method, whose linearisation is derived automatically.
