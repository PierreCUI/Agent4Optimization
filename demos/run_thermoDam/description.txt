Gravity Dam under Water Pressure, Seasonal Temperature and Self-Weight

This is the gravity dam problem of hydropower and flood control,
extended with the seasonal temperature field: a massive wall of
concrete holds back a reservoir by its own weight, the water presses on
the upstream face while the dam's weight pins it onto the rock
foundation, and at the same time a temperature difference stands across
the dam between the cold reservoir water and the sun-warmed air of the
downstream face, driving thermal stresses through the section. The dam
is a long prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is
treated as plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady: linear heat
conduction for the temperature T, measured in kelvin, and linear
elasticity for the displacement u, a vector with a horizontal and a
vertical component, measured in metres; the two are coupled one-way —
the temperature drives a thermal expansion strain of 3.6e-5 per kelvin
times the temperature, subtracted from the elastic strain, so a heated
solid region expands and pushes against its supports. The solid
material has Young's modulus 3.2e7 N/m^2, Poisson's ratio 0.23, density
2500 kg/m^3, thermal conductivity 1.25 W/(m K) and thermal expansion
coefficient 3.6e-5 per kelvin; regions without material are treated as
a weak phantom that is 1000 times softer, 1000 times less conductive
and 1000 times less expansive than the solid. The optimization starts
from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and
void cells covering the rectangle, roughly half solid, with a thin
solid strip along the top edge.

The left edge is the upstream face: the reservoir presses on it with a
water pressure that grows linearly with depth, zero at the top edge and
5000 N/m^2 at the bottom, pressing perpendicular to the face; the same
face is held at the fixed temperature 5, the cold reservoir water. The
bottom edge is the rock foundation: it is held in place by strong
penalty springs that pull its horizontal and vertical displacements back
to zero, and it is insulated, so that no heat crosses it. The right
edge, the downstream face, loses heat by convection to the surrounding
air at temperature 25, with a heat transfer coefficient h of 25 W/(m^2
K), the sun-warmed downstream air; mechanically it is free. The top
edge is insulated and free. Besides the water pressure, the solid
material is pulled downward by its own weight, a uniform body force of
24500 N/m^3 acting throughout the volume of every solid region; there
is no body heat. The pressurized upstream face and the foundation are
non-design boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen,
and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the downstream
face and the top edge are design boundaries, where material may be
attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the
elastic strain energy stored in the dam under the water pressure, the
seasonal temperature difference and its own weight — its compliance —
while using as little material as possible, a balance between stiffness
and material cost, and a strong penalty keeps the foundation from
sliding horizontally.
