Pad on Progressive Foundation Springs

This is the elastic foundation problem of railway sleepers with
progressive rail pads: a pad rests on soft ground that pushes back like
a mattress of springs which stiffen as they are compressed, and it
carries the rail seat or the machine standing on it, so the pad must
spread the load over the soil whose resistance grows with the
settlement. The pad is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid itself is a linear elastic material with Young's modulus
200 N/m^2 and Poisson's ratio 0.3; the nonlinearity sits in the ground
springs under the bottom edge: the ground pushes back vertically with
a restoring traction that grows faster than the settlement itself, the
spring stiffness being 100 N/m^3 at vanishing settlement and doubling
when the settlement reaches one tenth of a metre; this progressive
spring law makes the state problem nonlinear. Regions without material
are treated as a weak elastic solid 100 times softer than the real
material. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a
checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the whole
rectangle, roughly half solid.

The bottom edge rests on the ground: the ground
pushes back vertically with the progressive spring law described
above, while horizontally the pad may slide freely
over the soil; a short segment at the left end of the bottom edge, the
rail fastening, holds the pad against horizontal sliding. The top edge
carries the rail seat or the machine: a uniform traction of 10 N/m^2
pressing downward over the whole edge, perpendicular to it. The left
edge and the right edge are free. The ground-supported bottom edge,
its fastening segment and the loaded top edge are non-design
boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the two lateral edges
are design boundaries, where material may be attached or removed
freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy
stored in the pad under the top load — its compliance — while using
as little material as possible, a balance between stiffness and
material cost.
