Pressurized End Cap of Strain-Hardening Steel

This is the end cap problem of pressure vessels and boilers with
strain-hardening steel: a long cylindrical vessel is closed by a flat
plate welded to the cylinder at one end, the pressurised fluid inside
presses the cap outward, and the cap steel hardens wherever it
deforms most — in the bending zone near the welded rim and in the
bulging centre. The cap is treated as a long strip cut out between
two parallel symmetry planes through the vessel axis, so the problem
is plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid is a strain-hardening steel: its Young's modulus is not constant
but grows as the material deforms, being 200 N/m^2 where the material
is undeformed and doubling at a strain of about one seventh — the
modulus rises in proportion to the square of the local strain
magnitude; this dependence makes the state problem nonlinear.
Regions without material are treated as a weak
elastic solid 100 times softer than the solid at the same deformation
state. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial design: a
checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the whole
rectangle, roughly half solid.

The top edge is the inner face of the cap: the
pressurised fluid presses on it with a uniform pressure of 10 N/m^2
perpendicular to the edge, pressing into the cap. The bottom edge is
the welded rim: the cylinder wall holds it completely fixed, its
horizontal and vertical displacements both zero. The left edge and the
right edge are symmetry planes cutting the cap into a strip: on them
the horizontal displacement is held at zero while the vertical
displacement is free, a roller condition. The pressurized inner face
and the welded rim are non-design boundaries: their positions are
prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on them; the two symmetry planes are design boundaries, where
material may be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is
to minimize the elastic strain energy stored in the cap under the
internal pressure — its compliance — while using as little material as
possible, a balance between stiffness and material cost.
