Convectively-heated heat-sink topology optimization in two dimensions, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], with the design-dependent convection load of Iga et al. and in the spirit of the constructal cooling networks of Bejan (1997) [3]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of the conducting wall of a flue-gas heat-recovery duct scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. Low-temperature exhaust gas at 338 kelvin sweeps the central forty percent of the bottom edge and injects heat by convection through a Robin condition with a heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin — the gas side of a condensing heat-recovery exchanger; the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic. The bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the gas-side footprint is prescribed). The other three edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin, representative of the duct casing in ambient air. This problem is the convection-cooled heat-sink benchmark with its Neumann load boundary replaced by the Robin condition: the equivalent surface heat flux q_b = h_H (u_H - u_amb) = 25 x 40 = 1000 W/m^2 equals the benchmark flux q = 1000 W/m^2 by construction. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the gas-side surface-temperature integral weighted by q_b — the heat the heated side would absorb if it reached ambient temperature. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement.. The initial design is the full solid square with a circular hole of radius 0.1 cut out of its centre. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements; there is no Dirichlet condition, the temperature is fixed implicitly by the ambient air through the Robin condition.
