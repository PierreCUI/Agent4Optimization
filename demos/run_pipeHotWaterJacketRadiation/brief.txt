Nonlinear radiative district-heating-pipe heat-rejection topology optimization in two dimensions: the nonlinear version of the hot-water-jacket benchmark, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1], the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], and the radiative heat-transfer theory of Howell, Siegel & Menguc (2010) [3]. The design domain is the annular cross-section of a district-heating pipe: outer radius 0.5 m (a pipe of diameter 1 m, scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison), initial inner radius 0.1 m. The OUTER circle is a fixed boundary: the pipe casing exchanges heat with the environment at 298 kelvin through a Robin condition with effective convective heat transfer coefficient 25 W/(m^2 K) AND through surface radiation following the Stefan-Boltzmann law q_rad = eps sigma (u^4 - u_amb^4), where eps = 0.8 is the emissivity of the oxidised aluminium casing and sigma = 5.67e-8 W/(m^2 K^4) is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. At a casing temperature of order 345 kelvin the radiative flux is about 346 W/m^2, roughly one quarter of the convective flux — a physically realistic share for a hot district-heating jacket; the u^4 dependence makes the state problem NONLINEAR and is the physical mechanism studied here. The INNER circle is a design boundary — the tube wall whose shape is optimised — swept by the district-heating water at 353 kelvin, which injects heat by convection through a Robin condition with heat transfer coefficient 1000 W/(m^2 K), a realistic forced-convection coefficient for the water side of a heating network. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, 200 W/(m K), and the void region has the thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 W/(m K), i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the outer-surface temperature term weighted by q_b = h_H (u_H - u_amb) = 1000 x 55 = 55000 W/m^2 (the flux the inner wall would transfer to ambient if the surface reached ambient temperature); minimizing it therefore maximizes the heat rejected through the casing. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement. The initial design is a rotationally symmetric seed that covers both the heating wall and the casing while giving the optimiser enclosed interfaces to work from — a ring of evenly spaced holes on the mid-radius circle, one of the standard seeds of the pipeline's template library (the exact hole count and radius are numerical parameters chosen there, not part of the problem statement). The temperature field uses P2 finite elements; the nonlinear state equation is solved by Newton's method, whose linearisation is derived automatically.
