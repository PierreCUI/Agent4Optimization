Deep Beam on Two Supports

This is the simply supported deep beam problem of bridge deck girders
and mezzanine floor beams: a long girder spans between two supports
near its ends, a distributed load bears on its whole top, and all the
bending it takes must be carried down to the two supports. The girder
is a long prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is
treated as plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, linear elasticity
with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The solid is a
linear elastic material with Young's modulus 200 N/m^2 and Poisson's
ratio 0.3; regions without material are treated as a weak elastic solid
100 times softer than the real material. The optimization starts from a
prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void
cells covering the whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

There is no body force. The bottom edge carries the two supports: two
short segments near the bottom corners, each 0.2 m long, are the
bearing seats. The left seat holds both the vertical and the horizontal
displacement at zero, a pin support; the right seat holds only the
vertical displacement at zero and lets the beam slide horizontally over
it, a roller support; the remaining part of the bottom edge between the
seats is free. The top edge carries the deck load: a uniform traction
of 10 N/m^2 pressing downward over the whole edge, perpendicular to it.
The left edge and the right edge are free. The two support seats and
the loaded top edge are non-design boundaries: their positions are
prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on them; the free middle of the bottom edge and the two
lateral edges are design boundaries, where material may be attached or
removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain
energy stored in the beam under the deck load — its compliance — while
using as little material as possible, a balance between stiffness and
material cost.
