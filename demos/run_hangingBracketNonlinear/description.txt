Rubber Hanger with a Strain-Hardening Elastomer

This is the hanging bracket problem of vibration-isolation mounts: a
rubber-like bracket is bolted to the ceiling and carries a piece of
equipment suspended from its bottom edge, the elastomer — nearly
incompressible — deforms strongly under the hanging load and its own
weight, and it hardens as it stretches. The bracket is a long prism
whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane
strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid is a nearly incompressible rubber-like elastomer whose stiffness
grows as it deforms: its Poisson's ratio is 0.45, its Young's modulus
is 200 N/m^2 where the material is undeformed and doubles at a strain
of one tenth — the modulus rises in proportion to the square of the
local strain magnitude; this dependence makes the state problem
nonlinear. Regions without material are treated as a weak
elastic solid 100 times softer than the solid at the same
deformation state. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

The top edge is bolted to the ceiling: it is held completely fixed,
its horizontal and vertical displacements both zero. The bottom edge
carries the hanging equipment: a uniform traction of 10 N/m^2 presses
downward over its whole length, perpendicular to the edge. The left
edge and the right edge are free. Besides the hanging load, the solid
material is pulled downward by its own weight, a uniform body force
of 10 N/m^3 acting throughout the volume of every solid region. The
bolted top edge and the loaded bottom edge are non-design boundaries:
their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer
cannot add or remove material on them; the two lateral edges are
design boundaries, where material may be attached or removed freely.
The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy stored
in the bracket under the hanging load and its own weight — its
compliance — while using as little material as possible, a balance
between stiffness and material cost.
