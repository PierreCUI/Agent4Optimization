Induction-Heated Billet under a Press

This is the induction heating problem of hot forging and rolling: an
electromagnetic coil induces eddy currents inside a metal billet and
heats it volumetrically from within before the press or the rollers
shape it, the surface is cooled by water, and the press force bears
down on the billet. The billet is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady: linear heat
conduction for the temperature T, measured in kelvin, and linear
elasticity for the displacement u, a vector with a horizontal and a
vertical component, measured in metres; the two are coupled one-way —
the temperature drives a thermal expansion strain of 3.6e-5 per kelvin
times the temperature, subtracted from the elastic strain, so a heated
solid region expands and pushes against its supports. The solid
material has Young's modulus 3.2e7 N/m^2, Poisson's ratio 0.23, density
2500 kg/m^3, thermal conductivity 1.25 W/(m K) and thermal expansion
coefficient 3.6e-5 per kelvin; regions without material are treated as
a weak phantom that is 1000 times softer, 1000 times less conductive
and 1000 times less expansive than the solid. The optimization starts
from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and
void cells covering the rectangle, roughly half solid, with a thin
solid strip along the top edge.

Heat is generated uniformly inside every solid region, with a
volumetric heat source of 200 W/m^3 everywhere in the material, the
eddy-current heating of induction. The top edge loses heat by
convection to the cooling water at temperature 20, with a heat transfer
coefficient h of 2000 W/(m^2 K), the intensive water cooling of the
surface; the same top edge carries a uniform traction of 5000 N/m^2
pressing downward, the press force. The bottom edge is the anvil face:
it is insulated, so that no heat crosses it, and it is held in place by
strong penalty springs that pull its horizontal and vertical
displacements back to zero. The left edge and the right edge are
insulated and free. There is no body force. The cooled top face and the
anvil face are non-design boundaries: their positions are prescribed,
they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on
them; the two insulated lateral edges are design boundaries, where
material may be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is to
minimize the elastic strain energy stored in the billet under the press
force and the thermal expansion of the heated interior — its
compliance — while using as little material as possible, a balance
between stiffness and material cost, and a strong penalty keeps the
anvil face from sliding horizontally.
