Thermoelectric Leg between a Hot Plate and a Cold Plate

This is the thermoelectric leg problem of waste-heat recovery modules:
a leg stands between the hot exhaust plate and the water-cooled cold
plate of a thermoelectric generator, conducts the heat from the hot
side to the cold side, and is squeezed by the clamping force that
holds the module together. The leg is a long prism whose cross-section
is a rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady: linear heat
conduction for the temperature T, measured in kelvin, and linear
elasticity for the displacement u, a vector with a horizontal and a
vertical component, measured in metres; the two are coupled one-way —
the temperature drives a thermal expansion strain of 3.6e-5 per kelvin
times the temperature, subtracted from the elastic strain, so a heated
solid region expands and pushes against its supports. The solid
material has Young's modulus 3.2e7 N/m^2, Poisson's ratio 0.23, density
2500 kg/m^3, thermal conductivity 1.25 W/(m K) and thermal expansion
coefficient 3.6e-5 per kelvin; regions without material are treated as
a weak phantom that is 1000 times softer, 1000 times less conductive
and 1000 times less expansive than the solid. The optimization starts
from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and
void cells covering the rectangle, roughly half solid, with a thin
solid strip along the top edge.

The top edge is the hot junction face: it is held at the fixed
temperature 100, the hot exhaust plate, and it carries a uniform
traction of 5000 N/m^2 pressing downward, the clamping force of the
module assembly. The bottom edge is the cold junction face: it is held
at the fixed temperature 0, the water-cooled plate, and it is held in
place by strong penalty springs that pull its horizontal and vertical
displacements back to zero, for it seats on the rigid cold plate. The
left edge and the right edge are insulated, so that no heat crosses
them, and they are free of any mechanical load. There is no body heat
and no body force. The hot top face and the cold bottom face are
non-design boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen,
and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the two
insulated lateral edges are design boundaries, where material may be
attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the
elastic strain energy stored in the leg under the clamping pressure and
the thermal expansion of the hot side against the cold side — its
compliance — while using as little material as possible, a balance
between stiffness and material cost, and a strong penalty keeps the
cold face from sliding horizontally.
