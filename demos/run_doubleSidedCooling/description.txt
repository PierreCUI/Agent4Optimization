Double-Sided Cooling of a Power Module

This is the double-sided cooling architecture of power semiconductor
modules (IGBT or SiC): the die region, modeled as a uniform volumetric
heat source, is sandwiched between two cold plates, so heat leaves the
module through both the top and the bottom face, while the lateral
faces are encapsulated and insulated.

The problem is a topology optimization on a square plate of side 1 m.
The task is to find the best material layout inside the plate: solid
regions may be added or removed, and the design is described by a
level-set function, negative where the material is solid and positive
where it is void. The physics is steady, linear heat conduction with
one single physical field, the temperature T in kelvin. The solid
material has a thermal conductivity k of 1 W/(m K); regions without
material are treated as a weak heat conductor that conducts 10 times
less than the solid. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole plate, roughly half solid.

Heat is generated uniformly inside the plate, with a volumetric heat
source of 10 W/m^3 everywhere in the domain. The bottom edge and the
top edge are held at the fixed temperature 0: they are the two cold
plates; the left edge and the right edge are insulated, so that no
heat crosses them. Both cold edges are non-design boundaries: they are
frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the
two insulated lateral edges are design boundaries, where material may
be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize
the total thermal energy dissipated by heat conduction in the plate
while using as little material as possible — a balance between
conduction performance and material cost.
