Vessel End Cap under Internal Pressure

This is the end cap problem of pressure vessels and boilers: a long
cylindrical vessel is closed by a flat plate welded to the cylinder at
one end, the pressurised fluid inside presses the cap outward, and the
cap must carry that pressure across its whole span to the welded rim.
The cap is treated as a long strip cut out between two parallel
symmetry planes through the vessel axis, so the problem is plane
strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, linear elasticity
with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The solid is a
linear elastic material with Young's modulus 200 N/m^2 and Poisson's
ratio 0.3; regions without material are treated as a weak elastic solid
100 times softer than the real material. The optimization starts from a
prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void
cells covering the whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

There is no body force. The top edge is the inner face of the cap: the
pressurised fluid presses on it with a uniform pressure of 10 N/m^2
perpendicular to the edge, pressing into the cap. The bottom edge is
the welded rim: the cylinder wall holds it completely fixed, its
horizontal and vertical displacements both zero. The left edge and the
right edge are symmetry planes cutting the cap into a strip: on them
the horizontal displacement is held at zero while the vertical
displacement is free, a roller condition. The pressurized inner face
and the welded rim are non-design boundaries: their positions are
prescribed, they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on them; the two symmetry planes are design boundaries, where
material may be attached or removed freely. The optimization goal is
to minimize the elastic strain energy stored in the cap under the
internal pressure — its compliance — while using as little material as
possible, a balance between stiffness and material cost.
