Cold-plate-cooled power-module spreader: two-dimensional heat-conduction topology optimization following the benchmark formulation of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of the spreader plate of a liquid-cooled power module scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. A power chip occupying the central forty percent of the bottom edge injects a uniform heat flux of one thousand watts per square metre (0.1 W/cm^2); the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic. The bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the chip position is prescribed). The TOP edge — opposite the chip — is the wall of a cooling pipe (a cold plate, i.e. a liquid-cooled cold rail): it is held at the prescribed reference temperature of zero kelvin, the zero of the temperature scale, an isothermal heat sink of negligible thermal resistance. The left and right edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin. This is the cross-section of an electric-vehicle traction-inverter module: the dies load the baseplate, the opposite face is bolted to a water-glycol cold plate, and the sides see the air inside the housing. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the temperature integral over the chip — the thermal compliance that measures the thermal resistance of the spreader. The initial design is an I-beam (工字形) without holes: a full-width top flange (thickness 0.2) lying along the cooling wall, a bottom flange (length 0.5, thickness 0.2) under the chip footprint, and a web of width 0.2 running the full height between them. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements with the cooling-wall temperature applied directly to the wall segment; the remaining edges carry convective conditions.
