Cooling with a Temperature-Softening Thermal Interface Material

This is the thermal interface material (TIM) partial-contact problem of
pressure-contact power devices: the die's heat flux enters the package
through the left contact face, heat leaves through the cold plate on
the right, and a TIM (a phase-change thermal paste) covers only the
central segment of the top face — a partial-contact or delamination
defect. All other surfaces are insulated, so the design must route the
heat around the defective region to the intact cooling paths. The
paste is a phase-change material: as the interface heats up, the paste
softens and wets the mating surfaces better, so the TIM's effective
conductance improves precisely at the hottest spots.

The problem is a topology optimization on a square plate of side 1 m.
The task is to find the best material layout inside the plate: solid
regions may be added or removed, and the design is described by a
level-set function, negative where the material is solid and positive
where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear heat conduction with
one single physical field, the temperature T in kelvin. The solid
material has a thermal conductivity k of 1 W/(m K); regions without
material are treated as a weak heat conductor that conducts 10 times
less than the solid. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole plate, roughly half solid.

There is no internal heat generation: the whole left edge receives a
prescribed inward heat flux of 10 W/m^2, the die contact face where
heat enters; the right edge is held at the fixed temperature 0, the
cold plate; the central portion of the top edge, between 0.35 m and
0.65 m from the left corner, is covered by the TIM, losing heat by
convection to the surrounding air at temperature 0, and the TIM's heat
transfer coefficient is not constant: it improves as the interface
heats, being 1 plus 0.2 times the square of the local temperature in
kelvin, in W/(m^2 K), the softening of the phase-change paste — this
temperature dependence makes the state problem nonlinear; every
remaining part of the boundary — the bottom edge and the two uncovered
portions of the top edge — is insulated, so that no heat crosses it.
The flux-loaded left edge and the cold right edge are non-design
boundaries: they are frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on them; the TIM segment and all the insulated edges are
design boundaries, where material may be attached or removed freely.
The optimization goal is to minimize the total thermal energy
dissipated by heat conduction in the plate while using as little
material as possible — a balance between conduction performance and
material cost.
