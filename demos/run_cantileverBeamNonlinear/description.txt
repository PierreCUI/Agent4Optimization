Cantilever Beam with Strong Strain Hardening

This is the strongly hardening cantilever problem of long balconies
and flexible machine arms: a long slab or arm extends horizontally out
of a wall that holds it, carrying its own weight and whatever is
attached to its free end, and its material — soft for small strains —
hardens dramatically once the deflection grows large. The structure is
a long prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the problem is
treated as plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid is a strongly strain-hardening material: its Young's modulus is
not constant, being 200 N/m^2 where the material is undeformed,
changing little for small strains, and doubling at a strain of one
tenth — the modulus rises in proportion to the fourth power of the
local strain magnitude; this dependence makes the state problem
nonlinear. Regions without material are treated as a weak
elastic solid 100 times softer than the solid at the same
deformation state. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

The left edge is the wall face: it is held
completely fixed, its horizontal and vertical displacements both zero.
The right edge is the free tip, loaded across its whole length by a
uniform traction of 10 N/m^2 that pulls the beam horizontally outward,
perpendicular to the edge. The top edge and the bottom edge are free:
nothing is fixed and nothing loads them, so the beam may bend anywhere
along them. Besides the tip load, the solid material is pulled
downward by its own weight, a uniform body force of 10 N/m^3 acting
throughout the volume of every solid region. The fixed left edge is a
non-design boundary: its position
is prescribed, it is frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove
material on it; the loaded tip and the two free edges are design
boundaries, where material may be attached or removed freely. The
optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy stored in
the beam under the tip load and its own weight — its compliance —
while using as little material as possible, a balance between stiffness
and material cost.
