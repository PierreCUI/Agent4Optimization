Cooling-pipe heat-absorption topology optimization in two dimensions, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2]. The design domain is the annular cross-section of a cooling-water pipe: outer radius 0.5 m (a pipe of diameter 1 m, scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison), initial inner radius 0.1 m. The OUTER circle is a fixed boundary: the pipe casing exchanges heat with a warm environment at 298 kelvin through a Robin condition with effective convective heat transfer coefficient 25 W/(m^2 K). The INNER circle is a design boundary — the tube wall whose shape is optimised — held at the constant temperature 268 kelvin of the refrigerant evaporating inside the jacket, a typical evaporator temperature. Because moving-Dirichlet shape derivatives are not available, the constant-temperature condition is imposed as a PENALTY ROBIN condition with penalty coefficient pen = 1e15 W/(m^2 K): with k0 = 200 W/(m K) the boundary Biot number pen*r0/k0 ≈ 5e11 holds the inner wall within 1e-9 % of the prescribed temperature, and the label is a Robin design boundary listed in boundaryLabels. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, 200 W/(m K), and the void region has the thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 W/(m K), i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the casing-temperature integral weighted by q_b = h (u_amb - u_C) = 25 x 30 = 750 W/m^2 (the flux the inner wall would absorb from the environment if the surface reached ambient temperature); minimizing it keeps the casing cold, i.e. it maximizes the heat absorbed from the environment. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement. The initial design is a rotationally symmetric seed that covers both the cooling wall and the casing while giving the optimiser enclosed interfaces to work from — a ring of evenly spaced holes on the mid-radius circle, one of the standard seeds of the pipeline template library (the exact hole count and radius are numerical parameters chosen there, not part of the problem statement). The temperature field uses P2 finite elements.
