Unit Cell of a Periodic Microchannel Cooler with Temperature-Dependent Coolant Convection

This is the unit cell of a periodic microchannel heat sink or fin
array: the fin base at the bottom is a cold wall, the channel side on
the right is washed by the coolant and rejects heat by convection, and
the top and left edges are symmetry planes of the periodic array.
Designing one unit cell designs the whole periodic cooler — the
standard approach in microchannel heat exchanger design. The coolant
behaves like water: as the wall heats it, its viscosity drops, so the
heat transfer coefficient improves precisely at the hottest spots.

The problem is a topology optimization on a square plate of side 1 m.
The task is to find the best material layout inside the plate: solid
regions may be added or removed, and the design is described by a
level-set function, negative where the material is solid and positive
where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear heat conduction with
one single physical field, the temperature T in kelvin. The solid
material has a thermal conductivity k of 1 W/(m K); regions without
material are treated as a weak heat conductor that conducts 10 times
less than the solid. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole plate, roughly half solid.

Heat is generated uniformly inside the cell, with a volumetric heat
source of 10 W/m^3 everywhere in the domain. The bottom edge is the
fin base, held at the fixed temperature 0; the right edge is the
coolant-washed channel side, losing heat by convection to the coolant
at temperature 0, and the heat transfer coefficient is not constant:
it grows with the local wall temperature, being 1 plus 0.5 times the
wall temperature in kelvin, in W/(m^2 K), because the coolant's
viscosity falls as it warms — this temperature dependence makes the
state problem nonlinear; the top edge and the left edge are symmetry
planes, across which no heat crosses. The fin base and the two
symmetry planes are non-design boundaries: they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the convecting
channel side is a design boundary, where material may be attached or
removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the total thermal
energy dissipated by heat conduction in the plate while using as little
material as possible — a balance between conduction performance and
material cost.
