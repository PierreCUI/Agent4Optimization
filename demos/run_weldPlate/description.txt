Welded Plate under Local Heating and Tension

This is the welding distortion problem of structural fabrication: a
plate that works in tension is welded along a seam, the weld torch
pours a concentrated heat into a small zone of the plate, the plate
cools to the air, and the local expansion and contraction near the
weld pull the plate out of shape while it carries its working tension.
The plate is a long prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so the
problem is treated as plane strain in that cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 4 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady: linear heat
conduction for the temperature T, measured in kelvin, and linear
elasticity for the displacement u, a vector with a horizontal and a
vertical component, measured in metres; the two are coupled one-way —
the temperature drives a thermal expansion strain of 3.6e-5 per kelvin
times the temperature, subtracted from the elastic strain, so a heated
solid region expands and pushes against its supports. The solid
material has Young's modulus 3.2e7 N/m^2, Poisson's ratio 0.23, density
2500 kg/m^3, thermal conductivity 1.25 W/(m K) and thermal expansion
coefficient 3.6e-5 per kelvin; regions without material are treated as
a weak phantom that is 1000 times softer, 1000 times less conductive
and 1000 times less expansive than the solid. The optimization starts
from a prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and
void cells covering the rectangle, roughly half solid, with a thin
solid strip along the top edge.

Heat is generated inside every solid region by a localized volumetric
source concentrated at the centre of the rectangle, at the horizontal
position 2 m and the height 0.5 m, with the Gaussian profile
100000*exp(-((x-2)^2+(y-0.5)^2)/0.05^2) W/m^3, the weld torch heating
the seam. The top edge and the bottom edge lose heat by convection to
the surrounding air at temperature 20, with a heat transfer coefficient
h of 10 W/(m^2 K), and both are free of any mechanical load. The right
edge carries a uniform traction of 5000 N/m^2 pulling the plate
horizontally outward, its working tension, and it is insulated. The
left edge is the clamped end: it is held in place by strong penalty
springs that pull its horizontal and vertical displacements back to
zero, for the fixture grips the plate there, and it is insulated, so
that no heat crosses it. Besides the working tension, the solid
material is pulled downward by its own weight, a uniform body force of
24500 N/m^3 acting throughout the volume of every solid region. The
clamped left edge and the tensioned right edge are non-design
boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the convecting top and
bottom edges are design boundaries, where material may be attached or
removed freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain
energy stored in the plate under the working tension, the local weld
heating and its own weight — its compliance — while using as little
material as possible, a balance between stiffness and material cost,
and a strong penalty keeps the clamped end from sliding horizontally.
