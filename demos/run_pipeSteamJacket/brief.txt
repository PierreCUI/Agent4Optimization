Heating-pipe heat-rejection topology optimization in two dimensions, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], in the spirit of the constructal networks of Bejan (1997) [3]. The design domain is the annular cross-section of a steam-jacket pipe: outer radius 0.5 m (a pipe of diameter 1 m, scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison), initial inner radius 0.15 m. The OUTER circle is a fixed boundary: the pipe casing exchanges heat with the environment at 298 kelvin through a Robin condition with effective convective heat transfer coefficient 25 W/(m^2 K). The INNER circle is a design boundary — the tube wall whose shape is optimised — held at the constant temperature 373 kelvin of the saturated steam (1 atm) flowing inside. Because moving-Dirichlet shape derivatives are not available, the constant-temperature condition is imposed as a PENALTY ROBIN condition with penalty coefficient pen = 1e15 W/(m^2 K): with k0 = 200 W/(m K) the boundary Biot number pen*r0/k0 ≈ 7.5e11 holds the inner wall within 1e-9 % of the prescribed temperature, and the label is a Robin design boundary listed in boundaryLabels. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, 200 W/(m K), and the void region has the thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 W/(m K), i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the outer-surface temperature term weighted by q_b = h_H (u_H - u_amb) = 25 x 75 = 1875 W/m^2 (the flux the inner wall would transfer to ambient if the surface reached ambient temperature); minimizing it therefore maximizes the heat rejected through the casing. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement.. The initial design is a rotationally symmetric seed that covers both the heating wall and the casing while giving the optimiser enclosed interfaces to work from — a ring of evenly spaced holes on the mid-radius circle, one of the standard seeds of the pipeline's template library (the exact hole count and radius are numerical parameters chosen there, not part of the problem statement). The temperature field uses P2 finite elements.
