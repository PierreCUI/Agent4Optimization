Heat Spreading from a Small Contact Pad to a Cold Plate

This is the die-attach cooling problem of power chip packaging: the
chip's heat flux enters the substrate through a small contact pad on
one edge (the die-attach or solder joint area), and the substrate must
spread all that heat to the cold plate on the opposite edge. There is
no internal heat generation — the load is purely the boundary flux on
the contact pad. This is the classical point-contact heat-spreading
problem of power packaging.

The problem is a topology optimization on a square plate of side 1 m.
The task is to find the best material layout inside the plate: solid
regions may be added or removed, and the design is described by a
level-set function, negative where the material is solid and positive
where it is void. The physics is steady, linear heat conduction with
one single physical field, the temperature T in kelvin. The solid
material has a thermal conductivity k of 1 W/(m K); regions without
material are treated as a weak heat conductor that conducts 10 times
less than the solid. The optimization starts from a prescribed initial
design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering the
whole plate, roughly half solid.

There is no internal heat generation: a small contact pad on the
central portion of the left edge, between the heights 0.4 m and 0.6 m,
receives a prescribed inward heat flux of 10 W/m^2, so the chip's heat
enters the plate through this pad; the right edge is held at the fixed
temperature 0, the cold plate; every remaining part of the boundary —
the bottom edge, the top edge, and the two portions of the left edge
above and below the pad — is insulated, so that no heat crosses it.
The flux-loaded contact pad and the cold right edge are non-design
boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; all the insulated
edges are design boundaries, where material may be attached or removed
freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the total thermal energy
dissipated by heat conduction in the plate while using as little
material as possible — a balance between conduction performance and
material cost.
