Convection-cooled heat-sink topology optimization in two dimensions, following the heat-conduction topology optimization benchmark of Gersborg-Hansen, Bendsøe & Sigmund (2006) [1] and the formulation of Bendsøe & Sigmund (2003) [2], in the spirit of the constructal cooling networks of Bejan (1997) [3]. The design domain is the normalized unit square — the cross-section of an aluminium heat sink scaled to unit size for benchmark comparison. A power chip occupying the central forty percent of the bottom edge injects a uniform heat flux of one thousand watts per square metre (0.1 W/cm^2); the remaining bottom edge is adiabatic. The bottom edge is a fixed boundary (the chip position is prescribed). The other three edges exchange heat with ambient air at 298 kelvin through an effective convective heat transfer coefficient of twenty-five watts per square metre per kelvin, representative of a finned natural-convection heat sink; the implied chip temperature rise is on the order of a few kelvin. The design is encoded by a level-set function: the solid region has the thermal conductivity of aluminium, two hundred watts per metre per kelvin, and the void region has the actual thermal conductivity of air, 0.026 watts per metre per kelvin, i.e. the characteristic function takes the physically motivated contrast k_air/k_0 = 0.026/200 ≈ 1.3e-4. The objective minimizes the material volume, weighted by a Lagrange multiplier ℓ, plus the temperature integral over the chip — the thermal compliance that measures the thermal resistance of the heat sink. The Lagrange multiplier ℓ is a balance parameter of the optimisation, calibrated by the pipeline with a geometric scan that locates the knee of the volume-fraction curve; it is not part of the problem statement.. The initial design is the full solid square with a circular hole of radius 0.1 cut out of its centre. The temperature field uses P2 finite elements; there is no Dirichlet condition, the temperature is fixed implicitly by the ambient air through the Robin condition.
