Pad on an Elastic Foundation

This is the elastic foundation problem of railway sleepers and
machine-foundation pads: a pad rests on soft ground that pushes back
like a mattress of springs, and it carries the rail seat or the
machine standing on it, so the pad must spread the load over the soil
without sinking. The pad is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, linear elasticity
with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector with a
horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The solid is a
linear elastic material with Young's modulus 200 N/m^2 and Poisson's
ratio 0.3; regions without material are treated as a weak elastic solid
100 times softer than the real material. The optimization starts from a
prescribed initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void
cells covering the whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

There is no body force. The bottom edge rests on the ground: the
ground pushes back vertically with a restoring traction proportional
to the vertical displacement there, like a mattress of springs with a
stiffness of 100 N/m^3, while horizontally the pad may slide freely
over the soil; a short segment at the left end of the bottom edge, the
rail fastening, holds the pad against horizontal sliding. The top edge
carries the rail seat or the machine: a uniform traction of 10 N/m^2
pressing downward over the whole edge, perpendicular to it. The left
edge and the right edge are free. The ground-supported bottom edge,
its fastening segment and the loaded top edge are non-design
boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are frozen, and the
optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the two lateral edges
are design boundaries, where material may be attached or removed
freely. The optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy
stored in the pad under the top load — its compliance — while using
as little material as possible, a balance between stiffness and
material cost.
