Deep Beam with a Strain-Hardening Girder

This is the simply supported deep beam problem of bridge deck girders
made of strain-hardening steel: a long girder spans between two
supports near its ends, a distributed load bears on its whole top, and
the material itself grows stiffer wherever it deforms most, so the
hardening response participates in how the bending is carried down to
the supports. The girder is a long prism whose cross-section is a
rectangle, so the problem is treated as plane strain in that
cross-section.

The problem is a topology optimization on a rectangle 2 m wide and 1 m
tall. The task is to find the best material layout inside the
rectangle: solid regions may be added or removed, and the design is
described by a level-set function, negative where the material is solid
and positive where it is void. The physics is steady, nonlinear
elasticity with one single physical field, the displacement u, a vector
with a horizontal and a vertical component, measured in metres. The
solid is a strain-hardening steel: its Young's modulus is not constant
but grows as the material deforms, being 200 N/m^2 where the material
is undeformed and doubling at a strain of one tenth — the modulus
rises in proportion to the square of the local strain magnitude; this
dependence makes the state problem nonlinear. Regions without material
are treated as a weak elastic solid 100 times softer than the solid at
the same deformation state. The optimization starts from a prescribed
initial design: a checkerboard pattern of solid and void cells covering
the whole rectangle, roughly half solid.

The bottom edge carries the two supports: two short segments near the
bottom corners, each 0.2 m long, are the bearing seats. The left seat
holds both the vertical and the horizontal displacement at zero, a pin
support; the right seat holds only the vertical displacement at zero
and lets the beam slide horizontally over it, a roller support; the
remaining part of the bottom edge between the seats is free. The top
edge carries the deck load: a uniform traction of 10 N/m^2 pressing
downward over the whole edge, perpendicular to it. The left edge and
the right edge are free. The two support seats and the loaded top edge
are non-design boundaries: their positions are prescribed, they are
frozen, and the optimizer cannot add or remove material on them; the
free middle of the bottom edge and the two lateral edges are design
boundaries, where material may be attached or removed freely. The
optimization goal is to minimize the elastic strain energy stored in
the beam under the deck load — its compliance — while using as little
material as possible, a balance between stiffness and material cost.
